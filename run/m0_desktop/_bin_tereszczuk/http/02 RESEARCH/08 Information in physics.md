# Executive Summary: The Exhaustion of the Bio-Mechanical Model

For over 14 years, our research group-comprising academics and specialists from the **Warsaw University of Technology**, **IBM Poland**, the **Nature Laboratory**, and the **Subtle Energies Laboratory**-has pursued a rigorous experimental and theoretical program. Our central thesis is that modern science has reached a plateau due to the exhaustion of the traditional "bio-mechanical" model. 

While the last century of physics and biology has mastered the study of matter and energy, it has largely ignored **Information** as a fundamental constituent of the universe. We posit that Information is not merely a mathematical description of state (as per Shannon) but a physical driver of the functioning of complex systems. Our mission is to move these phenomena from the "paranormal" or "fringe" into the realm of rational, critical, and reproducible science.

# Experimental Evidence: From Microcosms to Agriculture

Our hypotheses are grounded in a series of long-term experimental programs that provide empirical weight to our claims.

## Aquatic Microecosystems (The Puchalski Series)

A cornerstone of our evidence base is the study of aquatic microcosms. In 2015, we conducted a 4-month experiment involving 30 variant complex aquatic microecosystems (5L each). These systems included producers (algae), consumers, and decomposers, mirroring the complexity of natural lakes.
* **The Intervention:** One half of the systems were treated with **"coded" aluminosilicates**-minerals acting as informational carriers.
* **Key Findings:** The "coded" variants exhibited significant shifts in metabolic efficiency. We observed increased functional diversity in algal assemblages and a marked increase in oxygen production (up to 5 mg L⁻¹ difference). 
* **Conclusion:** The introduced information acted not on individual species in isolation, but on the *interactions* between species, synchronizing their life processes and increasing the overall thermodynamic efficiency of the system.

## Informational Modification in Agriculture (The Zylbertal Series)

Between 2013 and 2015, we explored the application of informational modification to agricultural soil and hydroponic growth media.
* **Methodology:** Substrates were supplemented with material carriers designed to transmit biologically relevant signals through the water present in the soil and plant tissues.
* **Results:** Plants grown in "modified" substrates showed superior development and biomass accumulation compared to controls. Extended six-month studies suggested that these informational factors create a "memory" within the ecosystem, likely stored in complex organic compounds such as **hopanoids** (terpenoids produced by microorganisms).

## The Challenge of Repeatability: The "Operator Effect"

We acknowledge that our research is controversial. One of the primary criticisms leveled at research into "subtle information" is the difficulty of experiment replication in different laboratories. Our findings suggest a profound reason for this: **the consciousness of the operator is an integral component of the experimental system.**

In classical physics, the observer is assumed to be separate from the experiment. In the physics of information, this separation is an illusion. The state of the operator can determine whether the informational "signal" is successfully encoded or measured. 

### Stabilizing the Experimental Environment

To address this, we have developed protocols to control and standardize the "Operator Effect":
* **Brainwave Synchronization:** We have found that experiment success is significantly higher when the operator achieves a specific state of neurological coherence.
* **The Alpha Rhythm:** Synchronizing the operator’s dominant brainwave frequency to the **Alpha range (8-12 Hz)**-a state of relaxed but creative alertness-helps minimize mental "noise" that can interfere with the informational coupling of the experiment.
* **Hemispheric Synchronization:** Techniques to balance the activity of the left and right cerebral hemispheres allow the operator to act as a more stable "transceiving" element within the laboratory setup.

By treating the operator as a calibrated part of the measuring apparatus, we move closer to a methodology that can be taught and recreated by other researchers.

## Summary: Investigations of Information Load in Aquatic Microecosystems

This four-month study (2015) tested the impact of **coded aluminosilicate** on 30 diverse aquatic microecosystems. By comparing coded variants against uncoded controls across different pH levels and leaf types, the research observed how "informational load" alters biological behavior.

### Key Experimental Results:
* **Enhanced Decomposition:** The coded mineral significantly accelerated organic matter breakdown, particularly in **complex, mixed-leaf systems** (up to a 15% increase), while having little effect on simple, single-species setups.
* **Biological Shift:** Coded systems moved away from restrictive cyanobacterial films toward **oxygen-rich green algae and diatoms**, which support a healthier food chain.
* **Metabolic Efficiency:** Coded variants showed substantially higher photosynthetic activity, with dissolved oxygen levels increasing by as much as **5 mg/L** in some treatments.

### The "Complexity" Principle:
The study concludes that informational coding is most effective in **diverse environments**. It suggests that the information does not merely stimulate individual cells but **synchronizes species interactions**, leading to greater metabolic efficiency and ecosystem stability.

> **The Takeaway:** Coded information acts as a "biological synchronizer," proving most powerful in complex systems where it optimizes species interactions and accelerates natural life cycles.
